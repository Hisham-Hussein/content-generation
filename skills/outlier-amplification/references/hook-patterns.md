<overview>
The hook (lines 1-3, pre-fold on LinkedIn mobile) determines whether anyone reads the rest of the post. Hook analysis is a mandatory step in outlier decomposition, and hook sketching is a mandatory step in KI curation. A KI that can't support a strong hook is a weak KI.
</overview>

<hook_anatomy>
On LinkedIn mobile, the "see more" fold appears after approximately 2-3 short lines. The hook must:

1. **Stop the scroll** — trigger pattern interruption in the feed
2. **Create tension** — open a loop the reader needs to close
3. **Earn the click** — make "see more" feel mandatory, not optional

The blank line between line 1 and line 3 is strategic — it forces the fold earlier, ensuring the reversal or tension is hidden behind "see more."
</hook_anatomy>

<hook_patterns>

<pattern name="belief-reversal">
**Formula:** State a belief the audience holds (validating it) then demolish it with a single specific fact.

**Why it works for risk-averse audiences:** It respects their caution before showing them the evidence. They nod along on line 1, then get surprised on line 3.

**Structure:**
```
Line 1: [Common belief stated as fact — no "myth" or "think" framing]
Line 2: [blank]
Line 3: [Specific reversal with numbers, named entities, or scale proof]
```

**Example (Post 12):**
```
Everyone says AI can't handle complex legal work.

Tell that to the 4,000 lawyers using it daily across 43 countries.
```

**Dissection:**
- Line 1 uses "Everyone says" — creates social proof for the skepticism, making the reader feel safe agreeing
- Line 1 states belief as fact, not as myth — no "Think AI can't...?" or "Most people believe..."
- Line 3 uses "Tell that to" — confrontational but not aggressive. Tone of evidence, not argument
- Line 3 has three specificity anchors: "4,000 lawyers" (number), "using it daily" (production, not pilot), "43 countries" (scale)

**When to use:** When the audience holds a strong skeptical belief that data disproves. Best for Identity/Status and ROI registers.
</pattern>

<pattern name="data-shock">
**Formula:** Lead with a statistic that contradicts expectations or creates urgency.

**Structure:**
```
Line 1: [Unexpected statistic with specific numbers]
Line 2: [blank]
Line 3: [Context that makes the number personal/threatening/exciting]
```

**Example:**
```
100% of law firms say AI changed their pricing strategy.

34% have done anything about it.
```

**When to use:** When you have a hard number that creates cognitive dissonance. Best for Risk/Fear and ROI registers.
</pattern>

<pattern name="curiosity-gap">
**Formula:** Open a question or scenario the reader can't resolve without reading further.

**Structure:**
```
Line 1: [Intriguing scenario or partial revelation]
Line 2: [blank]
Line 3: [Deepen the mystery or add a surprising constraint]
```

**Example:**
```
A 6-person nonprofit team tripled its caseload last year.

They freed 43 people who spent a combined 622 years in prison.
```

**When to use:** When the story is inherently dramatic. Best for Ethics/Trust and Talent registers.
</pattern>

<pattern name="contrarian">
**Formula:** State an unpopular opinion with authority, backed by evidence.

**Structure:**
```
Line 1: [Bold claim that challenges conventional wisdom]
Line 2: [blank]
Line 3: [The evidence or credential that gives you authority to say this]
```

**Example:**
```
The billable hour isn't dying because of AI.

It's dying because Blackstone just invested $50M in a law firm that doesn't use them.
```

**When to use:** When you have institutional proof that validates the contrarian claim. Best for Identity/Status and Risk registers. Caution: requires strong credibility bridge or the audience dismisses it.
</pattern>

<pattern name="story-entry">
**Formula:** Drop the reader into a specific moment with sensory detail.

**Structure:**
```
Line 1: [Specific moment — "When [person] [did thing]..." or "Last [time], I..."]
Line 2: [blank]
Line 3: [The unexpected outcome or twist]
```

**When to use:** For personal experience posts or named-individual case studies. Less effective for data-driven content.
</pattern>

</hook_patterns>

<hook_dissection_process>
When analyzing an outlier's hook:

1. **Isolate lines 1-3** (pre-fold content)
2. **Classify the pattern** from the library above
3. **Identify the specificity anchors** — what exact words carry the most weight? (numbers, names, scale markers, status signals)
4. **Analyze the tone** — confrontational? Respectful? Authoritative? Curious?
5. **Map to audience psychology** — why did THIS pattern work for THIS ICP? What belief, fear, or identity did it target?
6. **Extract the transferable formula** — what structural elements can be replicated for different data?
</hook_dissection_process>

<hook_sketching_for_kis>
During Phase 4 (Curation), sketch a hook for each candidate KI using the pattern identified in Phase 1. This serves two purposes:

1. **Validation test:** If you can't write a strong hook from the KI's data, the KI may be too abstract or lack specificity. Consider dropping it.
2. **Post angle preview:** The hook sketch reveals which emotional register the post will naturally target, confirming register coverage.

Hook sketches are rough — 2-3 lines maximum. They're proof of concept, not final copy.
</hook_sketching_for_kis>
