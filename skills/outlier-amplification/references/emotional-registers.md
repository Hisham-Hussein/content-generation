<overview>
The 5-register framework ensures content diversity by mapping every Knowledge Item and post to a distinct emotional trigger. Without this framework, content defaults to ROI arguments and the audience tunes out.
</overview>

<registers>

<register name="roi-efficiency">
**What it triggers:** "This makes business sense."

**Audience state:** Rational evaluation mode. Looking for numbers, proof, ROI justification.

**Best data types:**
- Hard dollar savings or revenue figures
- Time savings with specific metrics (hours/week, percentage reduction)
- Named-firm case studies with before/after numbers
- Forrester TEI studies, industry benchmarks

**Hook patterns that work:**
- Data shock: Lead with an unexpected statistic
- Belief-reversal: "Everyone says X is expensive. [Firm] saved $Y."

**Risk of overuse:** If every post hits this register, the feed becomes a spreadsheet. Mix with Identity and Ethics posts.

**Example from Post 12:** "30% faster contract review across A&O Shearman. 360,000 hours saved annually at JPMorgan."
</register>

<register name="risk-fear">
**What it triggers:** "My competitor is ahead."

**Audience state:** Threat detection mode. Looking for signals they're falling behind.

**Best data types:**
- Adoption surveys showing what percentage of peers are already doing this
- Client expectation data (e.g., "64% of in-house counsel plan to cut outside firms")
- Competitive intelligence (AM Law 100 adoption rates)
- Market investment signals ($6B in legal tech funding)

**Hook patterns that work:**
- Competitive gap: "X% of your peers already do this. Here's what they know."
- Client pressure: "Your clients are planning to [threatening action]."

**Risk of overuse:** Fear fatigue. If every post screams danger, the audience goes numb. Space these 2-3 posts apart.

**Example:** "64% of in-house counsel expect to reduce outside counsel reliance. 59% don't even know if their firms use AI."
</register>

<register name="identity-status">
**What it triggers:** "Careful people like me are doing this."

**Audience state:** Social proof evaluation. Looking for evidence that people they respect have already moved.

**Best data types:**
- Named prestigious firms or individuals who adopted
- "First mover" narratives (A&O was the first enterprise Harvey deployment)
- Industry leader quotes
- Prestige signals (Harvard, Am Law 100, Fortune 500)

**Hook patterns that work:**
- Belief-reversal: "The most risk-averse companies on earth trust AI now." (Post 12's formula)
- Status signal: "When [prestigious entity] does X, the market follows."

**Why this register is powerful for risk-averse audiences:** It doesn't ask them to be brave. It tells them that cautious, careful people like them have already decided. This removes the identity threat of adoption.

**Example from Post 12:** The entire framing — "risk-averse companies" — was an identity play. It said: "You're careful. So were they. They moved anyway."
</register>

<register name="ethics-trust">
**What it triggers:** "I need to get this right."

**Audience state:** Governance and compliance mode. Looking for guardrails, best practices, cautionary tales.

**Best data types:**
- Regulatory guidance (ABA opinions, DIFC court rules, EU AI Act)
- Cautionary tales with lessons (Mata v. Avianca + the 3 guardrails)
- Governance frameworks (SDAIA, state bar guidelines)
- Court-issued AI rules

**Hook patterns that work:**
- Cautionary reversal: "The lawyer who cited fake cases — and the 3 guardrails that prevent it."
- Governance signal: "The most detailed AI rules for lawyers come from [unexpected source]."

**Why this register builds trust:** Ethics content positions the author as a responsible guide, not a hype merchant. It earns permission to make ROI claims in subsequent posts.

**Example:** Mata v. Avianca is the most viral legal AI story ever. But the flip side — what proper AI deployment looks like — is an enormous content gap.
</register>

<register name="talent-culture">
**What it triggers:** "This affects my people."

**Audience state:** Leadership and culture mode. Thinking about team impact, hiring, retention, skills gaps.

**Best data types:**
- Associate/employee survey data (Chambers 8,000-associate survey)
- Talent pipeline studies (Axios "AI threatens Big Law's talent pipeline")
- Work-life balance correlations with AI adoption
- Skills gap data, training investment ROI

**Hook patterns that work:**
- Counterintuitive: "8,000 associates reveal what makes a firm attractive. It's not pay."
- Existential: "Who trains the next generation when AI handles the grunt work?"

**Risk of overuse:** This register is niche — it resonates with managing partners and CLOs but less with individual contributors. Use sparingly (1 in 10 posts).

**Example:** Chambers Associate Survey found AI culture correlates with satisfaction independent of compensation — counterintuitive and engagement-generating.
</register>

</registers>

<register_coverage_planning>
When planning a campaign of 7-10 KIs, target this spread:

| Register | Target KIs | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| ROI/Efficiency | 2-3 | The foundation — business case content |
| Risk/Fear | 1-2 | Creates urgency without fatigue |
| Identity/Status | 1-2 | Removes adoption identity threat |
| Ethics/Trust | 1-2 | Builds credibility, earns permission for ROI claims |
| Talent/Culture | 1 | Differentiator, keeps feed from going stale |

Many KIs will map to 2 registers (e.g., ROI + Risk). That's fine — the primary register is what determines the hook angle, and the secondary adds depth to the body.
</register_coverage_planning>
