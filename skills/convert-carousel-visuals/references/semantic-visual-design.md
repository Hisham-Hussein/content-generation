# Semantic Visual Design

Convert the slide's claim, not merely its nouns.

## Concept test

Before generation, answer:

1. What causal relationship, comparison, hierarchy, sequence, or transformation does the slide assert?
2. What does each major visual element mean?
3. Could a NON-SPECIALIST member of the post's ICP recover the central relationship from the image alone, in about three seconds, with every annotation covered? "An informed viewer could work it out" is not a pass.
4. What plausible but incorrect interpretation could the image create?
5. Is a direct diagrammatic or physical representation clearer than the proposed metaphor?

Revise the concept when a major element exists only for atmosphere, when multiple objects share no defensible mapping, or when the image implies a relationship absent from the slide.

## Preferred mechanisms

Prefer:

- Inputs transforming into outputs.
- Diverging or converging paths.
- Layered systems and dependencies.
- Before-and-after state changes.
- Scaled comparisons.
- Human decisions acting on visible system state.
- Distinct capability workspaces or processes.

Use generic machinery, glowing brains, robots, floating interfaces, pods, and other familiar AI imagery only when their physical behavior directly maps to the claim. Novelty does not compensate for semantic weakness. Neither does intricacy: elaborate encoding is not artistry. And prefer a literal drawing of the slide's own content over an analogy: an analogy makes the reader translate between two worlds, so it only wins when it is genuinely clearer. What to avoid is any object the reader cannot name on sight, not the subject itself drawn plainly.

Brand categories require more than repeated logo holders. Represent the capability, operating model, integration, control model, or workflow difference associated with each brand, then add official marks deterministically.
