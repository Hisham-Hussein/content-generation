# Fat vs. Substance — the governing lens

Every rule in the other reference files is applied THROUGH this lens. Strunk's "omit
needless words" is about words that add nothing. It is never a license to remove
information. The whole tightening pass succeeds when the caption says the same things
in fewer words, and fails when it says fewer things.

**"Less text is better, but don't make it cryptic."** — the user's standing rule.

## Definitions

**Fat** — text whose removal loses zero information:
- Restatement: a line-block that says what the previous block already said in other words.
- Throat-clearing: setup sentences before the point ("Let me explain.", "Here's the thing
  most people miss." when a "Here's the…" signpost already opened the section).
- Hedges: "arguably", "in many cases", "tends to", "can often" — when the claim is one
  the post is willing to stand behind.
- Scaffolding: "What this means is", "The reason for this is that", "It's worth noting that".
- Empty intensifiers: "very", "really", "actually", "certainly", "literally".
- Detail lists nobody needed: three examples where one sharp one carries the point.
- The thesis word repeated as wallpaper (thin to ~3 load-bearing uses — a drumbeat,
  not wallpaper).

**Substance** — text whose removal loses information or identity. NEVER cut to make weight:
- Brand identity: iSemantics, the "we" agency voice ("we roll AI into companies…").
  Hisham has a team — never reframe toward solopreneur, and never use any "the teams …"
  scaffold (banned in every form).
- The hook's irony or contrarian angle — the reason the post stops the scroll.
- Hard numbers and named cases (L'Oréal 90→99.9%, Lyft −87%). A number is never fat.
- The KI source URL in the caption body.
- The framework itself — every pillar, phase, or step. Dropping one leg of a
  three-legged framework is amputation, not tightening.
- The closing beats: engagement question → save → repost → "And follow Hisham H. Shihab".
- The first-hand POV beat — the follow-worthiness lever. Tighten its wording, never
  delete the beat.

## The decision test

Before any cut, ask: **does the reader lose a fact, a beat, or a feeling — or only words?**

- Only words → cut directly. (Word-level trims need no approval.)
- A fact, a beat, a number, a whole line-block → it is substance-adjacent. Do NOT cut
  silently. Propose it to the user in the diff summary as an approve/reject item, with
  the char savings stated.

## Worked examples

Fat (cut on sight):

> "There is a reason why most founders never manage to make this shift."
> → "Most founders never make this shift." (saves 31 chars, loses nothing)

> "What this means in practice is that your team ends up waiting on you."
> → "Your team ends up waiting on you." (saves 36 chars)

> "Let me be clear: this is not about working harder. It's about working differently."
> → cut the first sentence entirely; the second (recast positively) carries it.

Substance (propose, never silently cut):

> "L'Oréal took resolution accuracy from 90% to 99.9%." — a hard number. Keep.
> "We saw this pattern again while rolling AI into a logistics firm last quarter." —
> the agency POV beat. Tighten wording if needed; the beat stays.
> "Full talk here: https://…" — the KI link. Untouchable.

## Voice-profile protections (override every Strunk rule)

These are ON-voice, not fat, even where a 1918 rule or a generic editor would flag them:

- Signature markers: ✅ ↳ ♻️ 👇 and differentiated list markers (✅ pillars, 1️⃣2️⃣3️⃣ phases).
- "Here's the… / here's the thing" signposts.
- Staccato fragments and one-line paragraphs ("Again and again he called out. No reply."
  — Strunk himself sanctions the emphatic fragment; the voice profile makes it signature).
- The two-line hook (≤60 chars per line, blank line between, line 2 deepens). The hook's
  CONTENT is out of scope entirely — never edit it in this skill.
